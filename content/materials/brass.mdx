## Overview

Brass is a copper-zinc alloy containing 5-45% zinc, producing a golden-colored metal that is stronger and more machinable than pure copper while retaining good corrosion resistance. The term "brass" covers a wide family of alloys; the most common industrial grade is 70/30 (cartridge brass, C26000) with 70% Cu and 30% Zn.

<Formula>ρ = 8500 kg/m³ for typical 70/30 yellow brass (varies 8400-8700 with Zn content)</Formula>

## Mechanical Properties

| Property | Value (70/30 annealed) |
|---|---|
| Density | 8,500 kg/m³ |
| Young's Modulus | 110 GPa |
| Yield Strength | 100 MPa (15,000 psi) |
| Ultimate Tensile | 320 MPa (46,000 psi) |
| Elongation | 65% |
| Hardness | 60-70 BHN |
| Melting Range | 915-955°C |

Higher-zinc brasses (40% Zn, Muntz metal) are stronger (up to 450 MPa) but less ductile.

## Key Properties

- **Machinability**: Free-cutting brass (C36000, leaded) is the benchmark for machinability (100% rating)
- **Formability**: 70/30 brass deep-draws extremely well — originally for cartridge cases
- **Corrosion resistance**: Good in atmosphere, fresh water; better than steel, slightly less than copper
- **Antimicrobial**: Inherent antimicrobial surface (like all copper alloys)
- **Joining**: Solders and brazes easily; weldable with proper filler
- **Appearance**: Polishes to a bright gold finish; lacquered for decorative use

<Callout type="info" title="Dezincification" severity="info">
Brass with &gt;15% zinc can suffer dezincification — selective zinc leaching that leaves porous copper and weakens the part — in stagnant, warm, or acidic water. Use dezincification-resistant (DZR) brass (C35200, C69100) for potable water plumbing fittings.
</Callout>

## Applications

- **Valves & fittings**: Gate/ball/globe valve bodies, plumbing fittings, compression fittings
- **Fasteners**: Screws, nuts, bolts, inserts (excellent thread rolling)
- **Hardware**: Locks, hinges, doorknobs, decorative trim
- **Electrical**: Connectors, terminals, socket contacts
- **Musical instruments**: Trumpets, trombones, cymbals, bells
- **Ammunition**: Cartridge cases (70/30)
- **Marine**: Propellers, through-hull fittings, underwater hardware (naval brass)
- **Plumbing**: Faucets, fixtures, stop valves

## Joining and Fabrication

- **Soldering**: Excellent with tin-lead or lead-free solders; standard for plumbing
- **Brazing**: Strong joints with silver brazing alloys
- **Machining**: Free-cutting brass (C36000) allows very high cutting speeds, produces small chips, excellent surface finish
- **Forging**: Forging brasses (C37700) used for valve bodies and fittings
- **Polishing**: Buffs to mirror finish; clear lacquer prevents tarnish

## Summary

Brass (density ~8500 kg/m³) adds zinc to copper for increased strength, machinability, and lower cost while retaining corrosion resistance and antimicrobial properties. It is the standard material for plumbing fittings, valves, decorative hardware, and high-volume machined parts. Free-cutting brass (C36000) is the reference material for machinability rating.

## Brass Weight Calculation

<Formula>W = V x rho (Volume x Density)</Formula>

Example: 1000 x 100 x 10mm brass (C360) plate:
- W = 0.001 m3 x 8500 kg/m3 = **8.5 kg**
- Compare: same copper = 8.96 kg (brass 5% lighter)
- Compare: same steel = 7.85 kg (brass 8% heavier)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the density of brass?**
Brass density varies by alloy: free-cutting brass (C360) is 8,500 kg/m3, cartridge brass (C260) is 8,530 kg/m3, and naval brass (C464) is 8,440 kg/m3. For general calculations, 8,500 kg/m3 is a good approximation.

**Is brass heavier than steel?**
Yes, brass (8,500 kg/m3) is about 8% heavier than carbon steel (7,850 kg/m3). However, brass is 5% lighter than pure copper (8,960 kg/m3).

**What is the best brass for machining?**
C360 free-cutting brass is the industry standard for machined parts — it has 100% machinability rating (the benchmark). Its 3% lead content provides excellent chip breaking and surface finish.
