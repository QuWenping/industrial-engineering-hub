## Overview

Type 316 is the second most common austenitic stainless steel, with molybdenum added (2-3%) to improve chloride and reducing-acid corrosion resistance. It is the grade specified for marine, coastal, chemical processing, and pharmaceutical applications. 316L is the low-carbon variant for welded construction.

<Formula>ρ = 8000 kg/m³, Composition: Fe-17Cr-12Ni-2.5Mo</Formula>

## Corrosion Resistance — Molybdenum Advantage

316's 2.5% molybdenum provides significantly better resistance than 304 to:
- **Chloride pitting/crevice corrosion**: PREN ≈ 25 (vs ~18 for 304)
- **Seawater and salt-laden coastal air**
- **Sulfuric and phosphoric acids**
- **Sulfurous atmospheres (pollution, pulp/paper)**
- **Many organic and fatty acids** (food processing)
- **Pharmaceutical and clean-room environments**

<Callout type="info" title="Pitting Resistance Equivalent Number" severity="info">
PREN = %Cr + 3.3×%Mo + 16×%N. Higher = better chloride pitting resistance:
- 304: ~18, 316: ~25, 317L: ~32, 2205 duplex: ~35, 254 SMO: ~43.
316 is suitable for seawater only below ~40°C; for warmer marine service use 2205 duplex or 6Mo alloys.
</Callout>

## Limitations

Despite better performance than 304, 316 still has limitations:
- **Chloride SCC**: Still susceptible above ~60°C in chloride environments
- **Continuous service above 870°C**: Sigma phase embrittlement possible
- **Hydrochloric acid**: Attacked even at dilute concentrations
- **Cost**: ~30-50% more expensive than 304

## Applications

- **Marine hardware** (boat fittings, dock hardware, propeller shafts)
- **Coastal architectural** (handrails, cladding, fasteners)
- **Chemical process equipment** (pumps, valves, heat exchangers)
- **Pharmaceutical manufacturing** (clean, non-contaminating)
- **Medical implants and devices** (surgical instruments, orthopedic)
- **Food processing** (salty/acidic products, soy sauce, brine)
- **Pulp and paper** (bleach plants)
- **Photographic and textile processing**

<Callout type="success" title="316L for Welded Process Piping" severity="tip">
316L (max 0.03% C) is the standard specification for welded process piping in chemical plants (ASTM A312 TP316L). It prevents intergranular corrosion in the weld heat-affected zone just like 304L prevents it for 304.
</Callout>

## Common 316 Variants

| Grade | Difference | Application |
|---|---|---|
| 316L | Low carbon (&lt;0.03%) | Welded construction |
| 316H | High carbon (0.04-0.10%) | High temperature (>500°C) |
| 316Ti | Titanium-stabilized | High temperature, welded |
| 316N | Nitrogen added | Higher strength |
| 316LVM | Vacuum melted | Medical implants |

## Fabrication Notes

- Weld with 316L filler (or 318 with higher Mo)
- Slightly higher springback than 304 in forming
- Slightly more difficult to machine than 304 due to work-hardening
- Passivate after machining/welding to restore corrosion resistance
- Non-magnetic in annealed condition

## Summary

Type 316/316L (density 8000 kg/m³, 2.5% Mo) is the marine and chemical-grade stainless steel with PREN ~25. It provides vastly better chloride and reducing acid resistance than 304. Use 316L for welded construction. Standard for coastal, marine, chemical, pharmaceutical, and food processing with salt/brine. The 30-50% cost premium over 304 is justified by extended service life in corrosive environments.
