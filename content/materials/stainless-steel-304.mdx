## Overview

Type 304 is the most common austenitic stainless steel — often called "18/8" for its 18% chromium / 8% nickel composition. It offers excellent general corrosion resistance, good formability, and ease of fabrication. 304 is the baseline stainless grade for food processing, architectural, chemical, and general industrial applications where moderate corrosion resistance is needed.

<Formula>ρ = 7930 kg/m³, Composition: Fe-18Cr-8Ni, max 0.08% C</Formula>

## Corrosion Resistance

304 provides excellent resistance to:
- Atmospheric environments (rural, urban, mild marine)
- Fresh water, steam
- Most organic chemicals, food products
- Oxidizing acids (nitric acid)
- Neutral and alkaline solutions

It is NOT suitable for:
- **Chloride environments** (seawater, brine, bleach) — causes pitting and crevice corrosion
- **Chloride stress corrosion cracking (SCC)** above ~60°C
- **Reducing acids** (hydrochloric, sulfuric at high concentrations)
- **Long-term exposure above 425°C** without low-carbon grade

<Callout type="warning" title="Use 304L for Welded Construction" severity="warning">
Standard 304 (max 0.08% C) becomes sensitized when held in the 425-870°C range (weld heat-affected zone). Chromium carbides precipitate at grain boundaries, causing intergranular corrosion. 304L (max 0.03% C) prevents this — always specify 304L for welded assemblies.
</Callout>

## Applications

- Food processing equipment, brewing, dairy
- Architectural trim, building facades, handrails
- Kitchen equipment, appliances, sinks
- Chemical tanks (non-chloride), pharmaceutical equipment
- Process piping for clean water, steam, non-chloride chemicals
- Heat exchangers, fasteners, springs

<Callout type="tip" title="304 vs 316 — When to Upgrade" severity="info">
Upgrade to 316/316L when: (1) coastal/marine installations, (2) process fluids contain chlorides, (3) chemical processing with reducing acids, (4) pharmaceutical clean service requiring higher purity. 316 costs ~30-50% more than 304.
</Callout>

## Fabrication Notes

- 304 work-hardens rapidly; deep drawing may require intermediate annealing
- Weld with 308L filler (over-alloyed by ~2% Cr/Ni)
- Post-weld cleanup (pickling paste or mechanical) restores corrosion resistance
- Non-magnetic in annealed condition (can become slightly magnetic after cold work)

## Summary

Type 304 (18/8, density 7930 kg/m³) is the general-purpose stainless steel for non-chloride environments. Use 304L for welded construction to avoid sensitization. Excellent formability, weldability, and general corrosion resistance. For marine, chloride, or reducing acid service, upgrade to 316L.

## Stainless Steel Weight Calculation

<Formula>W = V x rho (Volume x Density)</Formula>

Example: 1000 x 1000 x 10mm 304 plate:
- W = 0.01 m3 x 7930 kg/m3 = **79.3 kg**
- Compare: carbon steel = 78.5 kg (304 is 1% denser)
- Compare: aluminum = 27 kg (304 is 2.9x heavier)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the density of stainless steel 304?**
304 stainless steel has a density of 7,930 kg/m3, approximately 1% denser than carbon steel (7,850 kg/m3). 316 stainless is slightly denser at 7,990 kg/m3.

**Is 304 or 316 better for outdoor use?**
316 is better for marine and coastal environments due to its 2-3% molybdenum content, which provides superior pitting and crevice corrosion resistance. 304 is sufficient for most atmospheric and freshwater applications.

**What temperature can 304 stainless steel withstand?**
304 stainless steel can operate intermittently up to 870°C and continuously up to 925°C. It maintains good oxidation resistance due to its 18% chromium content, which forms a protective oxide scale.
