## SI Base Units

The International System of Units (SI) is the standard for engineering worldwide:

| Quantity | SI Unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length | meter | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Temperature | Kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Electric current | Ampere | A |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

US customary (Imperial) units are still widely used in US industries, oil/gas, and some manufacturing. Engineers must be fluent in both systems.

## Pressure

Pressure = force per area. SI unit = Pascal (Pa = N/m²).

| Unit | SI Equivalent | Common Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Pascal | 1 N/m² | Very small; use kPa or MPa |
| 1 bar | 100,000 Pa (100 kPa) | Standard industrial |
| 1 atm | 101,325 Pa | Standard atmospheric |
| 1 psi | 6,894.8 Pa | US customary |
| 1 kgf/cm² (at) | 98,067 Pa | Metric technical atm |
| 1 torr (mmHg) | 133.3 Pa | Vacuum measurements |
| 1 in H₂O | 248.8 Pa | Low pressure/drafts |
| 1 in Hg | 3,386 Pa | Barometric pressure |
| 1 mm Hg | 133.3 Pa | Medical/lab |

<Formula>1 bar = 14.50 psi = 0.987 atm = 29.53 in Hg = 10.2 m H₂O = 750 torr</Formula>

## Temperature

| Scale | Freezing Water | Boiling Water | Absolute Zero |
|---|---|---|---|
| Celsius (°C) | 0 | 100 | -273.15 |
| Fahrenheit (°F) | 32 | 212 | -459.67 |
| Kelvin (K) | 273.15 | 373.15 | 0 |
| Rankine (°R) | 491.67 | 671.67 | 0 |

### Conversions

<Formula>°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9</Formula>
<Formula>°F = °C × 9/5 + 32</Formula>
<Formula>K = °C + 273.15</Formula>
<Formula>°R = °F + 459.67</Formula>

<Callout type="warning" title="Temperature Differences" severity="warning">
Temperature DIFFERENCE: ΔT of 1°C = ΔT of 1 K = ΔT of 1.8°F = ΔT of 1.8°R. When converting heat transfer coefficients or temperature differences, don't add/subtract 32 — only apply the scaling factor (5/9 or 9/5).
</Callout>

### Standard/Gas Flows

Gas flows are often referenced to standard conditions (1 atm, specified temperature):
- **Nm³/h**: Normal cubic meters per hour (0°C, 1.013 bar abs)
- **SCFM**: Standard cubic feet per minute (60°F, 14.7 psia)

1 Nm³/h = 0.622 SCFM (approximately — depends on gas composition)

Convert actual flow to standard:
<Formula>Q<sub>std</sub> = Q<sub>actual</sub> × (P<sub>actual</sub>/P<sub>std</sub>) × (T<sub>std</sub>/T<sub>actual</sub>)</Formula>

## Quick Conversion Cheat Sheet

### To Convert US to SI (Memorize These)

1. **psi → bar**: ÷ 14.5
2. **bar → psi**: × 14.5
3. **°F → °C**: (°F−32) × 5/9
4. **gpm → m³/h**: × 0.227
5. **ft → m**: × 0.305
6. **lb → kg**: × 0.454
7. **hp → kW**: × 0.746
8. **Btu → kJ**: × 1.055
9. **in → mm**: × 25.4
10. **mile → km**: × 1.61

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick Mental Math" severity="info">
For rough estimates: bar ≈ psi / 15 (close enough for site work). m ≈ ft / 3 + 5% (10 ft ≈ 3m). kg ≈ lb / 2 + 10% (100 lb ≈ 45 kg). °C ≈ (°F − 30)/2 for ambient temps (70°F ≈ 21°C, actual is 21°C — very close!).
</Callout>

## Summary

Engineering calculations require consistent units — always convert to SI (or one consistent system) before plugging into formulas. The SI system uses meters, kilograms, seconds, Kelvin, and Pascals. Critical conversion factors: 1 bar = 14.5 psi = 100 kPa, 1 m³ = 264 US gallons, °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9, 1 hp = 0.746 kW. Temperature differences use the 9/5 or 5/9 factor without offset. Always check units in formulas — unit errors cause some of the most expensive engineering mistakes.
